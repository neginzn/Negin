# 30 DAYS TRAPPED INSIDE AN ALIEN SPACESHIP (v2 — edited)

DAY 1

You open your eyes.

You have thirty days to get off this ship.

You just don’t know it yet.

You’re trapped inside a rectangular chamber surrounded by a glowing blue laser field. White lights shine above you, and strange machines surround the room.

You try to move, but you can’t get out.

A few meters away, you notice Jax.

He’s sitting on a metal chair, with his wrists and upper body tied to it.

“Jax!”

He slowly opens his eyes.

“Ray…?”

Neither of you remembers how you got here.

The last thing you remember is escaping the aliens on Earth. Then a bright light… and nothing.

Jax starts struggling against the restraints.

He twists his wrists, trying to create enough space to free one hand. After several attempts, he manages to loosen the restraint and pull himself free.

A useful trick when your hands are restrained is to avoid pulling wildly; creating a little slack first can make it easier to work the fastening loose.

Jax rushes toward your chamber.

There’s a control panel beside it.

He presses the button.

The blue laser field disappears.

You step out, still weak and confused.

You look around. This isn’t a hospital.

It’s a laboratory.

Outside, strange figures move through the corridors.

You’re inside an alien spaceship.

And you have no idea how long you’ve been here.

You find two protective suits inside a storage cabinet. Since you don’t know whether the ship’s atmosphere is safe for humans, wearing them is the sensible choice.

The suits have their own oxygen supply, temperature control, and carbon dioxide filters. A small display on your wrist confirms that the life-support systems are working.

You put them on.

Just then, the laboratory door opens.

An alien walks inside.

You and Jax immediately hide behind a large metal cabinet.

The alien stops.

Its eyes move across the room. A strange display appears in its vision.

HUMAN DETECTED.

You hold your breath.

Then you see it.

Jax’s elbow is sticking out from behind the cabinet.

Slowly, you pull his arm back behind the metal.

SIGNAL LOST.

The alien waits for a moment.

Then it looks toward the other side of the room and continues walking.

You don’t move until the door closes.

You have survived your first encounter.

And you have learned your first rule.

If any part of you is visible, they can scan you.

DAY 2

You and Jax stay inside the laboratory.

You explore carefully, searching for anything useful.

The display on your wrist runs a test of the air.

AIR: BREATHABLE.

You take off your helmets and save the suit oxygen for later.

You might need it.

Near a workstation, you find an identification card. It has an alien symbol on it.

You hold it against the scanner beside the lab door.

The door responds.

You keep it.

It might be useful later.

But there's a bigger problem.

There's no water in this laboratory.

DAY 3

A human can survive around three weeks without food, but only about three days without water.

This is day three.

You have to leave the laboratory.

You use the card and step into a narrow side corridor.

Above the main corridor, you notice a small black dome with a red light. A security camera.

So you stay in the quiet side passages.

The ship also has security cameras in important areas, especially main corridors, laboratory entrances, and the hangar.

You need to be careful about where you move.

At the end of the side corridor, the air gets colder.

You enter a cooling section, full of humming machines and frosted pipes.

Behind one of the machines, you find a tank of clean water used to cool the equipment. About twenty liters.

You fill your bottles and drink slowly.

Back in the laboratory, you begin to understand how the aliens detect humans.

From what you've observed, you learn a few important things:

* Their eyes can scan humans and display information about them.
* A solid object, like a wall or cabinet, can block their scan.
* Every part of your body has to stay behind it.
* Darkness alone won't protect you if they can still see you.
* They react to sudden noises, bright lights, and movement.

You write these rules down on a piece of paper.

DAY 5

While exploring a quieter section of the ship, you discover a large room behind a locked door.

You use the identification card.

The door opens.

Inside, you see rows of transparent capsules.

There are humans inside them.

Men, women, even children.

Most appear unconscious.

You and Jax walk between the capsules, trying to understand what the aliens are doing with these people.

Then someone moves.

A man inside one of the capsules opens his eyes.

You find a control panel beside him. After a few attempts, you manage to release the capsule.

The man falls forward, coughing.

“My name is Daniel,” he says.

He tells you that he was abducted eleven days ago.

The aliens forced him to work in the lower maintenance sections of the ship.

He knows some of its layout, including the location of the hangar.

But when he hears how you and Jax woke up, he looks confused.

“Wait… you were both in separate laboratory chambers?”

You nod.

Daniel looks back at the capsules.

“Then why were you treated differently?”

Nobody has an answer.

DAY 8

Three people need more supplies than two.

By the morning of the eighth day, the water tank is almost empty.

You search a storage room and find sealed ration packages, compressed food blocks, and three containers of drinking water. Forty-five liters.

The food blocks are dense and dry, designed to provide plenty of energy in a small package. Each one contains around 500 calories.

One block each per day is only about a quarter of what an adult body needs. Enough to survive. Not enough to stay strong.

They’re not exactly a delicious meal, but right now, nobody is expecting a restaurant.

Jax reaches into his pocket.

He finds a small chocolate bar.

He looks at it for a moment.

Then he breaks it into three pieces.

You and Daniel eat yours.

Jax puts his piece back in his pocket.

“Later.”

You start dividing the remaining supplies into daily portions.

You write down the remaining supplies and agree not to eat more than your daily portions.

You don’t know how long you’ll be trapped here.

And wasting food now could cost you later.

DAY 10

Daniel tells you about a maintenance passage near the power section.

He says it connects to a lower level of the ship.

You follow him to a metal panel behind a row of machines.

He removes a few screws and pulls the panel away.

Behind it is a narrow rectangular passage used for ventilation and cables.

But there’s a problem.

A large ventilation fan is running inside.

Crawling through it while the fan is active would be extremely dangerous.

Daniel points to a control switch.

You wait until the fan stops completely.

Then you crawl through, one at a time.

The passage is uncomfortable and covered in dust, but it leads to another section of the ship.

At the other end, you find a small observation window.

Beyond it is the hangar.

A shuttle is parked inside.

It’s your way out.

But several aliens are guarding it.

You need a plan.

DAY 12

For the next two days, you observe the guards from a safe distance.

You notice something important.

The aliens change positions at regular intervals.

During the shift change, the main corridor stays empty for a short time.

You write down the times on your paper.

When the next shift change begins, you leave the maintenance area and quietly enter a security room.

Inside, several monitors show different sections of the ship.

You find a map of the lower levels.

More importantly, you discover that the hangar’s emergency door can be released manually from the power room.

But opening it will trigger an alarm.

You now know what you need to do.

You just need to get there without being detected.

📊 DAY 12 | PEOPLE: 3 | FOOD: 8 DAYS | WATER: 27 L | OXYGEN: SHIP AIR OK

DAY 15

You reach the power room during another shift change.

The room is filled with electrical equipment, control panels, and thick cables.

Daniel finds the emergency release.

You activate it.

A loud alarm immediately fills the ship.

Red lights begin flashing.

SECURITY ALERT.

You hear footsteps outside.

An alien enters the room.

You and Jax hide behind a large machine. Daniel crouches behind a metal equipment cabinet.

Every part of you behind the metal.

The alien slowly looks around.

Its eyes scan the room.

A display appears in its vision.

AREA SCANNED.

NO HUMAN DETECTED.

The alien pauses.

Then it turns and leaves.

You wait several seconds before moving.

But the plan has gone wrong.

The hangar door didn’t open.

Instead, every corridor door on the lower level locks at once.

The ship is in lockdown.

The guards double.

You can’t reach the hangar.

For the next three days, you hide inside the maintenance passage, eating less than your portions and drinking only when you have to.

DAY 18

On the third day, the alarm finally stops.

The guards go back to their normal positions.

Daniel knows that the lower-level ventilation system connects to the hangar section.

You remove the metal panel again.

Before entering, you make sure the fan is switched off.

This time, you crawl farther than before.

Eventually, you reach another control area near the hangar.

Daniel reconnects a loose cable and activates the backup release.

A heavy door begins to open.

The sound echoes through the lower level.

You freeze.

Somewhere nearby, an alien responds to the noise.

You can hear it approaching.

You move as quickly and quietly as possible.

The passage leads directly toward the hangar.

And now the way is open.

But your last bottle of water is empty.

📊 DAY 18 | PEOPLE: 3 | FOOD: 2 DAYS | WATER: 0 L | OXYGEN: SHIP AIR OK

DAY 20

You finally reach the hangar.

The shuttle is still there.

But one alien guard is standing near the entrance.

You wait behind a large support column.

When the guard turns away, you use the identification card on the shuttle’s access panel.

The door opens.

All three of you climb inside.

Under the seats, you find an emergency kit.

Thirty liters of water. Thirty food blocks.

Jax drinks first. Nobody stops him.

Daniel moves toward the control panel.

He has worked near the maintenance systems, but he has never flown a shuttle like this.

Still, he understands enough of the controls to get the engines started.

The display lights up.

OXYGEN: LOW
POWER: LIMITED
FLIGHT SYSTEM: READY

You look at each other.

There is only enough power for one serious attempt.

Daniel searches the navigation system.

You select Earth as the destination.

For a moment, everything seems fine.

Then the display changes.

DESTINATION: MARS

You stare at the screen.

Jax turns toward you.

“What?”

You look at Daniel.

“Why does it say Mars?”

Daniel tries to change the destination.

The controls don’t respond.

Then an alarm begins.

UNAUTHORIZED DEPARTURE DETECTED.

The hangar doors start closing.

An alien appears outside the shuttle.

It looks directly toward you.

Its eyes begin to scan the cockpit.

Daniel pushes the throttle forward.

The shuttle launches just before the doors close.

The alien ship disappears behind you.

For the first time in twenty days, you’re free.

But you’re not going home.

📊 DAY 20 | PEOPLE: 3 | FOOD: 10 DAYS | WATER: 30 L | OXYGEN: LOW

DAY 22

The destination is locked.

Daniel can’t override it.

A human spacecraft needs about seven months to reach Mars.

This shuttle says it will get there in nine days.

You still don’t know why the aliens were taking you there.

Or why you and Jax were placed in separate laboratory chambers instead of the human capsules.

But right now, there’s a bigger problem.

The shuttle’s oxygen is running out faster than the trip.

The more you move, the faster you breathe. Panic and movement use far more oxygen than a body at rest.

So you make a decision.

You shut down every system you don’t need. The heating. The lights. The screens.

The cabin turns cold.

You put the suits back on.

The suit oxygen you saved on day two is now the reason you can breathe.

You ration the water to one liter each per day.

Less than a body wants. But enough to stay alive for a while.

And then you do the hardest thing.

Nothing.

You stay still. You breathe slowly. You wait.

DAY 27

The shuttle begins shaking.

ENGINE PERFORMANCE: UNSTABLE.

A large red planet fills the window.

Mars.

The air on Mars is about one percent as thick as Earth’s.

That’s why slowing down is the hardest part of landing there. There’s almost nothing to push against.

The shuttle enters the atmosphere.

The heat shield glows as the outside temperature rises.

Daniel says the remaining power may not be enough for a normal landing.

You tighten your seat restraints.

Jax looks at you.

He reaches into his pocket.

The piece of chocolate he saved on day eight.

He breaks it in half and gives you one.

“Later is now.”

📊 DAY 27 | PEOPLE: 3 | FOOD: 3 DAYS | WATER: 9 L | OXYGEN: SUITS 20%

DAY 29

CONTROL SYSTEM FAILURE.

The shuttle begins spinning.

Daniel shouts that the engines are no longer responding.

The surface of Mars is rushing toward you.

A final warning appears.

EMERGENCY LANDING IMPOSSIBLE.

Daniel tries one last time to regain control.

Nothing happens.

The shuttle hits the ground.

Metal tears apart.

The entire cabin shakes.

Then everything goes black.

DAY 30

Silence.

You slowly open your eyes.

Your ears are ringing.

The shuttle is broken.

Smoke fills the cabin.

You try to move your arms.

You’re alive.

You look toward Jax.

He’s still breathing.

Then you hear Daniel coughing behind you.

All three of you survived the crash.

But when you look through the broken window, you see nothing familiar.

Red sand.

Rocks.

A dark sky.

You’re not on Earth.

You’re on Mars.

No food.

No water.

And almost no air.

And now, you have thirty days to survive.
