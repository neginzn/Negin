# Delphi — اسکریپت صاحب کانال، نسخه‌ی ۳ (۲ فصل طولانی‌تر و ۱ صحنه‌ی بازجویی) (با اصلاحات واقعیت و جای کلیپ‌ها، ۳ اکتبر ۲۰۲۶)

> تغییرات نسبت به نسخه‌ی صاحب کانال با ✏️ در جدول پایین فایل آمده. 🎬 = جای کلیپ واقعی.

CH 1 — Five Words · 0:00
September 21st, 2026. A courtroom inside the Indiana State House, three judges. On one side, the state of Indiana. On the other, the lawyers of a man serving 130 years for killing two girls. The prosecutor explains why Allen was kept alone in a cell for more than a year, and one of the judges looks at her and says five words.
🎬 [appeal hearing — Judge Brown: "You protected him into psychosis."]
For nine years, this case has been one of the most famous in America. And right now, it's not over. To understand why, we have to go back to a bridge.

CH 2 — One Afternoon · 0:45
February 13th, 2017. Delphi, Indiana, a small town where most people know each other. Abby Williams is 13, Libby German is 14, and they're best friends. That afternoon, Libby's older sister drops them off at the trail. They walk toward the Monon High Bridge, an old railroad bridge high above the creek, with wide gaps between the boards. Libby takes a photo of Abby on the bridge and posts it. That's the last thing anyone hears from them. When nobody shows up at pickup time, the families start searching, and the next day, the girls are found in the woods near the creek.
That's everything the town knows. Well, almost everything. Because Libby's phone was still recording.

CH 3 — Down the Hill · 2:00
On that phone, police find a few seconds of video. A man walking behind the girls on the bridge, a blurry figure, hands in his pockets, head down. And a voice. Three words.
🎬 [Bridge Guy video — "Guys… down the hill."]
Police release the image and the audio to the whole country, and the internet gives him a name: Bridge Guy.
🎬 [police press conference, 2017]
Thousands of tips come in. Then two sketches: one of an older man, and later, a completely different one of a much younger man. Nobody understands why they look nothing alike.
April 2019. Two years in, the head of the Indiana State Police steps up to the microphone, and he talks straight to the killer.
🎬 [press conference, April 22nd, 2019 — Doug Carter: "We believe you are hiding in plain sight."]
He says police believe this man is from Delphi, that he lives there, or works there, or visits all the time. Remember that line. Because it turns out he was right. Months pass, then years. Five years go by, and Bridge Guy is still just a shadow on a bridge.

CH 4 — The File in the Wrong Place · 3:30
And here's where this case turns. Because Bridge Guy wasn't missing. He was in the file the whole time.
Three days after the murders, a local man meets a conservation officer in a grocery store parking lot and tells him, on his own, that he was on that trail that afternoon. He says he walked toward the old bridge, sometime between 1:30 and 3:30, and that he passed three girls on the way. The officer writes it down, the note gets marked "cleared," and then it just sits there. For five years.
His name is Richard Allen. He works at the CVS in town, and he lives a few minutes away.
In 2022, a volunteer going through old files finds that note, and the sheriff reads one line again and again. Three girls. Because that afternoon, three teenage girls really were on that trail, and they had passed a man. Years later, one of them would sit in court and say, "That was the man I had waved at." Nobody outside the investigation knew about those three girls. So how did Allen?
And notice the time. Allen says he was there from 1:30 to 3:30. Libby's video was recorded at 2:13. Right in the middle.
Investigators go back to him. That's what we know about Richard Allen. Plus one more detail. He owns a .40 caliber pistol. And at the crime scene, between the girls, police had found one unfired .40 round. The state's expert says it was cycled through his gun.
October 2022. Richard Allen is arrested.
🎬 [arrest press conference, October 31st, 2022]
Bridge Guy finally has a name. Or does he?

CH 5 — Thirteen Months Alone · 5:15
October 13th, 2022. Allen sits down with investigators, and right away they tell him he's free to leave at any time. That's not kindness. It's a technique. If a man is free to go and still chooses to stay, everything he says counts. And he stays. He admits he was on that trail. But the moment they push, he gets defensive.
🎬 [interrogation, October 13th, 2022]
And notice what he says. Not "I didn't do it." He says, "I don't want to be associated with this thing any more than everyone else does." Then, when the pressure keeps coming, he tells them: arrest me, or take me home.
After the arrest, he isn't sent to a local jail. He's held, for his own safety, in a maximum-security prison, in solitary confinement, for 13 months. And inside that cell, something changes. His lawyers say he slips into psychosis. And during that time, he starts confessing. To his wife on the phone, to his mother, to people around him. Dozens of times.
🎬 [prison phone call — confession]
And notice the contrast. Outside, a quiet pharmacy worker who denies everything. Inside, a man confessing again and again. So which one is the real Richard Allen? That's the question this whole appeal is about.

CH 6 — What the Jury Never Heard · 7:00
His defense had another story. They said the girls were killed by a group, in some kind of ritual, linked to a belief called Odinism. The judge didn't allow it in front of the jury.
And there's more. One witness who saw a man near the bridge that day described him as someone in his twenties, with brown, curly hair. Allen was 44 at the time. And that younger sketch police spent two years showing the whole country? The jury never saw that either.

CH 7 — 130 Years · 8:30
October 2024. The trial finally begins. After seventeen days of testimony, the jury deliberates for about 19 hours, over four days. November 11th: guilty on all counts. In December, the judge gives him the maximum. 130 years.
For the families, after almost eight years, it's over. Or at least, that's what everyone thinks.

CH 8 — The Appeal · 9:30
Allen's new lawyers file their appeal, with three arguments. One: the search of his house was illegal. Two: his confessions came out of a mind broken by solitary confinement, so they can't count. Three: he was never allowed to tell his side of the story.
The state answers every point. Allen was kept alone to protect him, because he was a target. His condition changed after a religious conversion, and after he saw the evidence against him. Some guards believed he was faking. And, the state says, remember who first put Allen on that trail, at that exact time. Allen himself.

CH 9 — "You Protected Him" · 11:30
September 21st, 2026. Back in that courtroom. And the judges don't sound like they've made up their minds. They ask why those two very different sketches were never shown to the jury. They push hard on what happened to Allen in that cell. And when the prosecutor says the isolation was for his protection, Judge Elaine Brown answers with those five words.
🎬 [appeal hearing — "You protected him into psychosis."]
The hearing ends. No ruling, and no deadline.

CH 10 — Waiting · 12:30
So what happens now? Most likely, nothing changes, because very few convictions are ever reversed on appeal. But if this one is, the case goes back, and prosecutors would have to decide whether to try Richard Allen all over again.
Nine years later, two families are still waiting. And somewhere in a prison cell in Oklahoma, so is Richard Allen.
But whatever the judges decide, one thing will never change. On that bridge, a fourteen-year-old girl saw a man walking behind her, and she pressed record. Everything in this case started with her.
So tell me: was this trial fair? Write it in the comments. And when the ruling comes, I'll be back with the update.
